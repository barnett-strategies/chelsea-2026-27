Still a projection, but Friday's pre-match press conference moved it. The shape is unchanged — a 3-4-2-1 with Sánchez behind an Acheampong-Lacroix-Colwill back three, Gusto and Hato as wing-backs, Palmer and Morgan Rogers working off João Pedro — and the single change from the earlier version is in central midfield: Moisés Caicedo comes in for Roméo Lavia alongside Reece James. Alonso said on 28 August that Caicedo, who has missed Chelsea's opening two fixtures with a training injury picked up last week, was due to train that day: “He is hopefully training. We are hopeful he can be involved. We are still checking; no decision made.” That is a long way short of a guarantee, and “involved” is not the same word as “starts” for a player with no minutes this season, so treat this slot as the coin-flip it is — Roméo Lavia is the fallback if Caicedo is held back for the bench. Wesley Fofana is now formally available for the first time this season, his cross-competition suspension fully served, but Levi Colwill trained on Friday with no lasting concern and the back three that opened the season is intact, so Fofana projects as the first rotation option rather than a starter. Not everyone agrees: Yahoo's post-press-conference XI does start Fofana, which tells you how open that third centre-back slot really is. Geovany Quenda, like Colwill, was left out of Thursday's cup squad and also trained Friday — Alonso flagged no issue with either. Two genuinely new items since the last update, and both cut against the certainty of this XI. First, Enzo Fernández. Alonso left him out of the squad for Thursday's EFL Cup tie and insisted the call was sporting rather than commercial: “It was just because of the game. Enzo is a good professional. He's training well.” Sky Sports nonetheless report that Chelsea are prepared to sell to Manchester City at the right price — an asking price of up to £120m, a City bid expected before the window closes at 11pm BST on Tuesday 1 September, and Enzo Maresca driving the interest from the City bench. The deadline falls two days after this match so Fernández is selectable, but a midfielder being actively negotiated over 48 hours out is not a safe pick for a starting XI, which is why he is not in this one. Second, the goalkeeper, and this is the field most likely to be wrong by Sunday lunchtime. Chelsea have agreed a £7.5m deal with Aston Villa for Emiliano Martínez — two years plus a one-year option — and ESPN's read is that he becomes the No. 1. The medical and registration still have to clear, and Alonso said he has already decided his goalkeeper for Sunday but declined to name him. Robert Sánchez therefore stays in this projection by default rather than by conviction. Cole Palmer is fine; Alonso played down any suggestion of resting him and he is expected to start. Marco Palestra and Jordan Henderson (wrist, sustained at the World Cup, targeted to return around 6 September at Arsenal) remain out, Emanuel Emegha is still injured, and Aaron Anselmino, Caleb Wiley and Mamadou Sarr are variously out or doubtful.
