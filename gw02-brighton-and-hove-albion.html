Still a projection, and Saturday's crop of previews hardened the disagreements rather than settling them. The XI here is unchanged from Friday — a 3-4-2-1 with Sánchez behind an Acheampong-Lacroix-Colwill back three, Gusto and Hato as wing-backs, Caicedo alongside Reece James, and Palmer and Morgan Rogers working off João Pedro — but three of its slots are genuinely open and it is worth being blunt about which.

The goalkeeper is still the field most likely to be wrong by Sunday lunchtime. Emiliano Martínez is settled and out of the picture: Football365's updated preview reconfirms he will not be registered in time for this match despite the £7.5m deal, so whoever starts, it is not him. The live question remains Robert Sánchez versus Mike Penders, and Saturday moved the count the wrong way for the name in this XI — 3 Added Minutes joined the Evening Standard in projecting Penders, making it two published XIs for Penders against Football365's flat statement that Sánchez is expected to start. Sánchez is kept here on the same two grounds as before: he is the established No. 1 with 32 appearances last season, and he is the only name a source will commit to in prose rather than a graphic. But Alonso said he has already decided his goalkeeper and then declined to name him, which is not usually how a manager behaves when the answer is the incumbent. Treat this as close to a coin flip.

Central midfield is the second coin flip. Chelsea's own site carries Alonso saying he is hopeful Moisés Caicedo — who has missed both fixtures so far with a training injury — can be involved, with no decision made and a late assessment expected. Football365 reads the same briefing more cautiously, as Caicedo possibly returning to the matchday squad, which is a bench call rather than a start. For a player with no minutes this season, that is the more conservative reading and it may well be right. Roméo Lavia is the fallback alongside James, with Valentín Barco the second alternative.

The third open call is the left of the back three and the left wing-back, and here the published XIs are actually against this one. Both the Evening Standard and 3 Added Minutes start Wesley Fofana ahead of Levi Colwill and prefer Pep Chavarría to Jorrel Hato. Colwill and Hato are kept because of what Chelsea's own site reports Alonso saying: Colwill and Geovany Quenda were left out in midweek deliberately, to manage their load after long injury absences last season, with no issue behind it, and both are in contention. A manager who rests a defender in midweek is usually planning to start him at the weekend. Two of three previews going the other way is not a comfortable margin, though, and Fofana is now formally available for the first time this season — Chelsea's own site and Football365 both confirm the two-match cross-competition ban is fully served. One preview still lists him as suspended; that is stale, not a live dispute.

Enzo Fernández is the item that moved most since Friday, and it moved against him playing. Chelsea are now reported to be preparing for a fresh formal Manchester City bid before the window shuts at 11pm BST on Tuesday 1 September — City let Chelsea's 14 August deadline for the £120m asking price pass without an offer, but have since agreed to sell Nico González to Newcastle, and Fernández is reported as open to reuniting with Enzo Maresca. Alonso insisted leaving him out of the midweek squad was a sporting call — "It was just because of the game. Enzo is a good professional. He's training well" — while also saying "My wish is that we know what will happen for the future but we will see this week." Football365 now reads him as a bench option at best and possibly out of the squad entirely. The deadline falls two days after this match so he is selectable, but a midfielder being actively negotiated over is not a safe pick for a starting XI, which is why he is not in this one.

Cole Palmer is fine and expected to start; Alonso played down any suggestion of resting him. Marco Palestra and Jordan Henderson (wrist, fractured at the World Cup and surgically repaired, targeted to return around 6 September at Arsenal) remain out, Emanuel Emegha is still out with a hamstring strain, and Aaron Anselmino, Caleb Wiley and Mamadou Sarr are variously out or doubtful.

Alonso's first home game in charge. The rescheduling question is now settled: the Premier League's August-September fixture-amendment notice confirms this as Sunday 30 August, 14:00 BST, moved to accommodate Brighton's UEFA Conference League playoff tie the preceding Thursday. Chelsea have an EFL Cup tie against Luton on Thursday 27 August, three days before this one, so rotation is a live factor. Brighton arrive short of forwards: Yankuba Minteh (leg) is out for several weeks, Evan Ferguson (ankle) is not expected back until late October or November, Stefanos Tzimas (knee) is targeted for early-to-mid October, and Kaoru Mitoma is still working back from summer hamstring surgery — Hürzeler says he is getting closer but he is not available here. Jack Hinshelwood picked up a training injury and Hürzeler called the weekend likely too soon for him; Georginio Rutter faces a late fitness test and is the more likely of the two to feature. New signings Promise David and Jaouen Hadjam are expected to be options, and Pascal Struijk and Ferdi Kadioğlu are back in the squad.
