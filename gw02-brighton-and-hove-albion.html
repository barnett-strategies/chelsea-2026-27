Still a projection, but Saturday evening's previews moved it twice — once back toward the name we had, and once decisively away from two others. Two slots have changed since this morning.

The goalkeeper has swung back to Robert Sánchez, and this is the clearest news of the day. Saturday morning the count read two published XIs for Mike Penders against one prose source for Sánchez, and this page said so. By Saturday evening it had reversed: Sports Mole's preview, updated at 16:14 UK, states plainly that Sánchez is expected to retain the role, Al Jazeera's Saturday preview picks him, and Football365 already had him. The two Penders XIs — the Evening Standard's and 3 Added Minutes' — are both Friday pieces that have not been refreshed. Newer sources beat older ones, so Sánchez stays and the confidence behind him is higher than it was this morning. The Emiliano Martínez question is closed: the £7.5m deal is agreed but he cannot be registered in time, so he is not an option here. Alonso still says he has decided his goalkeeper and still will not name him, so this is not settled — but it is no longer a coin flip.

Wesley Fofana comes into the back three, replacing Josh Acheampong. This is a correction, not a hunch: every published XI we can find — Sports Mole, Al Jazeera, the Evening Standard and 3 Added Minutes — starts Fofana, and this projection was the only one that did not. His two-match cross-competition ban, carried over from a red card on the final day of last season, is fully served and Chelsea's own site confirms he is available competitively for the first time this season. The two Saturday previews drop Acheampong to make room; the two Friday ones drop Colwill instead. We follow the newer pair and keep Colwill, because Alonso's own explanation supports it — Colwill and Geovany Quenda were left out in midweek deliberately to manage their load after long injury absences, with no issue behind it, and both are in contention. A manager who rests a defender on purpose is usually planning to start him.

Pep Chavarría replaces Jorrel Hato at left wing-back for the same reason, and here the evidence is unanimous: all four published XIs pick Chavarría. He signed from Rayo Vallecano this month after 33 LaLiga appearances last season and has barely featured yet, so this is a projection built on what previewers expect rather than on anything he has done in a Chelsea shirt. Hato is the obvious alternative if Alonso prefers continuity.

Moisés Caicedo is kept in the XI but the start-or-bench call is genuinely open. He has missed both fixtures so far with a training injury; Chelsea's own site has Alonso hopeful he can be involved, with no decision made and a late assessment expected, and reporting since confirms he is back in training. Sports Mole and Al Jazeera both start him. Football365 reads the same briefing more cautiously — available for the squad but unlikely to start — which for a player with no minutes this season may well be the right call. Roméo Lavia is the fallback alongside James, with Valentín Barco a second option.

Enzo Fernández remains out of this XI and the case for leaving him out has only firmed up. Manchester City are preparing a fresh formal bid before the window closes at 11pm BST on Tuesday 1 September: they let Chelsea's 14 August deadline for the £120m asking price pass without an offer, but have since agreed to sell Nico González to Newcastle, and Fernández is reported as open to rejoining Enzo Maresca. Alonso called the midweek omission a sporting decision — "It was just because of the game. Enzo is a good professional. He's training well" — while adding "My wish is that we know what will happen for the future but we will see this week." Football365 and Sports Mole now both read him as a bench option at best, possibly out of the squad entirely. The deadline falls two days after this match so he is selectable; a midfielder being actively negotiated over is still not a safe pick.

Cole Palmer is fine and expected to start — Alonso played down any idea of resting him. Marco Palestra and Jordan Henderson (wrist, fractured at the World Cup and surgically repaired, targeted to return around 6 September at Arsenal) remain out, Emanuel Emegha is still out with a hamstring strain, and Aaron Anselmino, Caleb Wiley and Mamadou Sarr are variously out or doubtful. No official team sheet exists until roughly an hour before Sunday's 14:00 BST kickoff.

Alonso's first home game in charge. The rescheduling question is settled: the Premier League's August-September fixture-amendment notice confirms this as Sunday 30 August, 14:00 BST, moved to accommodate Brighton's UEFA Conference League playoff tie the preceding Thursday. Chelsea also had an EFL Cup tie against Luton on Thursday 27 August, three days before this one, so rotation is a live factor. Brighton arrive short of forwards: Yankuba Minteh (leg) is out for several weeks, Evan Ferguson (ankle) is not expected back until late October or November, Stefanos Tzimas (knee) is targeted for early-to-mid October, and Kaoru Mitoma is still working back from summer hamstring surgery — Hürzeler says he is getting closer but he is not available here. Jack Hinshelwood picked up a training injury and Hürzeler called the weekend likely too soon for him. Georginio Rutter has been in a protective boot since the Aston Villa fixture and faces a late fitness test; he is the more likely of the two to feature but is now a genuine doubt rather than a probable. New signings Promise David and Jaouen Hadjam are expected to be options, and Pascal Struijk and Ferdi Kadioğlu are back in the squad. The practical read: Brighton are missing four of their forward options and may be without a fifth, so Chelsea's back three should face a less direct attacking threat than Brighton's league position suggests.
