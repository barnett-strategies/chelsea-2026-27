Team news

Three of the projected eleven missed the open portion of Tuesday's training session — Reece James, João Pedro and Moisés Caicedo. Only the media-visible part of the session was observed, so this is not confirmation that any of them is injured, but it is the reason this projection carries a health warning.

Caicedo is the longest-running absence. He has a calf problem and has now sat out several fixtures. Alonso's public line before matchweek 4 was that he was not yet willing to take a risk — *"Let's see for Brentford, but unluckily not at the moment to take risks with him. Not yet, we are not there yet."* Most previews still list him as out.

The genuinely good news is at the other end of the squad. Jordan Henderson has returned to full training after his World Cup injury and Marco Palestra is back as well; both are described as closing in on a Chelsea debut, and Friday is a realistic date for one or both off the bench. Emmanuel Emegha and Dastan Satpaev remain unavailable.

Brentford are missing a chunk of their defence. Nathan Collins, Sepp van den Berg, Josh Dasilva and Antoni Milambo are all listed out, with Mathias Jensen a doubt, and Keith Andrews' side played a midweek cup tie on Tuesday — so some rotation is likely.

What changed from the projection

This projection is the matchweek 4 eleven, repeated. That is a deliberate call rather than laziness: two of the three current previews name exactly that side, and Alonso has not signalled a change. It also means the page is carrying the same back three — Fofana, Colwill, Hato — that Alonso picked ahead of Lacroix and Acheampong last time out, after this page spent a week arguing about the wrong defenders.

The contested spots are both on the left. Sports Mole projects Maxence Lacroix back into the back three for Hato, and Pep Chavarría at left wing-back, which would push Pedro Neto over to the right and Malo Gusto out. The other two sources keep Gusto at right wing-back with Neto on the left. Call that a 2-1 split, not a settled question.

The largest risk to this eleven is fitness, not selection. If James or João Pedro does not recover, the shape stays but the personnel move — the Chelsea Chronicle floats Valentín Barco in midfield and Danny Welbeck through the middle. Treat the front three as the most secure part of the projection and the midfield pair as the least.

The tactical read

Expect the 3-4-3 that Alonso used at Stamford Bridge last time, with Palmer and Rogers tucked in behind João Pedro rather than holding width. KhelNow frames the same personnel as a 4-2-3-1, which is less a disagreement than a description of what the shape becomes in possession when one wing-back pushes on and the other holds.

The midfield pair is the pressure point. With Caicedo still out, Lavia and James are being asked to screen a back three away from home against a side that plays fast, direct football. Lavia has an injury history and James is a converted full-back in that role — Chelsea have no natural third holder available if either fades.

Set pieces are the tactical subplot. Chelsea recruited set-piece coach Austin MacPhee specifically to address a known defensive weakness, and Brentford are the single worst possible opponent to still be working on it — the long throw into the box remains a core part of how they generate chances. A taller back three of Fofana, Colwill and Hato helps.

What to watch

Whether James and João Pedro are actually on the team sheet. Both missed Tuesday's visible training. The official XI lands an hour before kickoff and will settle it — if either is absent, this whole projection shifts.

Which side Pedro Neto starts on. He switched to the left in matchweek 4 when Gusto came in at wing-back. If Chavarría starts instead, Neto goes back to the right. It is the quickest read on which of the two competing projections Alonso agreed with.

Brentford's long throws and second balls. This is the clearest test yet of whether the MacPhee appointment has changed anything about how Chelsea defend a crowded box.
