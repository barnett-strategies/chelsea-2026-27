Team news

Alonso ruled nobody out. At Thursday's press conference — his last before kickoff — he said: *"In terms of injuries and the squad, we are having the last training session today. Nobody is ruled out for Friday. We will wait until tomorrow to make the final decision."* That is deliberately unhelpful, and it means this page goes into Friday carrying the same question marks it had on Wednesday.

Reece James and João Pedro both missed the open portion of Tuesday's session, which is what started the worry. Every source read since has them available as things stand, and Alonso's blanket answer did not contradict that. Both are treated here as likely to start.

Caicedo's position has softened. The line this page carried before matchweek 4 was that Alonso would not risk him — *"not at the moment to take risks with him. Not yet, we are not there yet."* On Thursday that became *"He's getting closer and tomorrow we will make the final decision."* He still has not started a match this season, so he stays out of the projected eleven, but he is now a live possibility rather than a safe absence. Jordan Henderson (recovering from a wrist procedure), Marco Palestra and Emmanuel Emegha are all described as in contention for a squad place — in contention, not confirmed fit.

Brentford are missing a chunk of their defence. Nathan Collins, Sepp van den Berg, Josh Dasilva and Antoni Milambo are all listed out, with Mathias Jensen a doubt, and Keith Andrews' side played a midweek cup tie on Tuesday — so some rotation is likely.

What changed from the projection

The projection has not moved, and Thursday gave no reason to move it. This is still the matchweek 4 eleven, repeated — the same back three of Fofana, Colwill and Hato that Alonso picked ahead of Lacroix and Acheampong last time out. Two of the three previews on file name exactly that side, and the press conference added nothing to argue with.

The contested spots are both on the left, and both are still open. Sports Mole projects Maxence Lacroix back into the back three for Hato, and Pep Chavarría at left wing-back, which would push Pedro Neto over to the right and Malo Gusto out. The other two sources keep Gusto at right wing-back with Neto on the left. Call it a 2-1 split. Note that all three of those previews were published on 16 September, before the presser — none of them had more information than this page does.

One correction to what this page said on Wednesday. It described Henderson and Palestra as having returned to full training and closing in on a debut, with Friday a realistic date. That was too strong. The sourcing supports only that both are candidates for the matchday squad, and Thursday's reporting still lists them among the doubts. The team news above reflects the weaker, accurate version.

The largest risk to this eleven remains fitness, not selection. If James or João Pedro does not recover, the shape stays but the personnel move. The front three is the most secure part of this projection; the midfield pair is the least.

The tactical read

Expect the 3-4-3 that Alonso used at Stamford Bridge last time, with Palmer and Rogers tucked in behind João Pedro rather than holding width. KhelNow frames the same personnel as a 4-2-3-1, which is less a disagreement than a description of what the shape becomes in possession when one wing-back pushes on and the other holds.

The midfield pair is the pressure point. As projected, Lavia and James screen a back three away from home against a side that plays fast, direct football. Lavia has an injury history and James is a converted full-back in that role. If Caicedo is passed fit late, this is where he lands and the whole balance of the team changes — he is the only natural holder in the building.

Set pieces are the tactical subplot. Chelsea recruited set-piece coach Austin MacPhee specifically to address a known defensive weakness, and Brentford are the single worst possible opponent to still be working on it — the long throw into the box remains a core part of how they generate chances. A taller back three of Fofana, Colwill and Hato helps.

What to watch

Whether Caicedo is on the team sheet at all. Alonso moved him from *"not yet"* to *"getting closer"* in the space of a week and put the decision on Friday morning. Even a place on the bench would be the most meaningful team-news development of the season so far.

Whether James and João Pedro are actually named. Both missed Tuesday's visible training. The official XI lands an hour before kickoff and settles it — if either is absent, this projection shifts at both ends.

Which side Pedro Neto starts on, and Brentford's long throws. Neto switched left in matchweek 4 when Gusto came in; if Chavarría starts instead, Neto goes back right. And the crowded box is the clearest test yet of whether the MacPhee appointment has changed how Chelsea defend one.
