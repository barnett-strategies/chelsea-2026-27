Team news

Alonso ruled nobody out, and matchday has not changed that. At Thursday's press conference — his last before kickoff — he said: "No-one is ruled out for us for Friday. I prefer to be cautious and we will wait until tomorrow to take the final decision." That tomorrow is today. Every call goes to this morning's final training session, and the official team sheet an hour before kickoff settles it.

João Pedro is a genuine maybe, not a probable absentee. He missed Tuesday's open session and has withdrawn from the Brazil squad for the coming international window. Alonso met that inference head-on rather than letting it stand: "We will see tomorrow if João is ready to play, it's a possibility," confirming the Brazilian could play here while still missing the trip. Yesterday this page called him the most likely name to drop out of the eleven; the manager's own words are softer than that. Danny Welbeck is the named fallback if he does not make it. Cutting the other way: a two-week international break starts straight after this match, so there is no congestion argument for risking him — which is a scheduling reason to sit him, not a medical one.

Reece James looks fine; Caicedo still does not. On James's Tuesday absence Alonso said it was "just a decision for Tuesday and we see his development and tomorrow we will decide" — precautionary management, not an injury, and he is treated here as likely to start. Moisés Caicedo has gone from "not at the moment to take risks with him" before matchweek 4 to "He's getting closer" on Thursday, but he has played 14 minutes all season after his calf problem recurred, and one rushed comeback has already cost Chelsea. He stays out of the projection. Jordan Henderson (wrist) and Marco Palestra are both reported as pushing for involvement with debuts possible if cleared, but the sourcing is split on whether they are actually available. Treat a debut as possible, not expected.

Brentford are missing four and doubtful on a fifth. Nathan Collins (calf), Sepp van den Berg (groin), Josh Dasilva (thigh), Antoni Milambo (knee) and Kafe Furo are all listed out, with Mathias Jensen doubtful on fitness. Two of those are centre-backs. Keith Andrews' side also played a midweek cup tie on Tuesday, so rotation is likely on top of the absences.

What changed from the projection

The projection has changed this morning for the first time all week: Lacroix in, Gusto out. Until today this page carried the matchweek 4 eleven unaltered, with Jorrel Hato as the left-sided centre-back and Malo Gusto at right wing-back. Both of Friday's fresh previews instead put Maxence Lacroix into the back three and move Hato out to left wing-back, pushing Pedro Neto to the right and leaving Gusto out of the side. Sports Mole has been pointing that way since Wednesday. Three of five sources now agree, including both of today's, so the projection has been changed to follow the freshest evidence rather than the oldest.

This is the least secure call on the page, and Gusto is the reason. The two Wednesday previews still name the unchanged matchweek 4 side, and Read Chelsea keeps Gusto in its own version even while promoting Lacroix. The back three feels like the firmer half of the change; which of Gusto or Neto occupies the right flank is close to a coin toss. If the official sheet has Gusto starting, this page called it wrong.

What has held up all week is everything else. Martínez in goal, Fofana and Colwill in the back three, Lavia and James in midfield, Palmer and Rogers behind the striker — no source has wavered on any of it, and Thursday's press conference produced no selection steer to move it. The spine is the secure part of this projection.

One correction to yesterday's framing. This page graded João Pedro as the name most likely to drop out, reasoning from his Brazil withdrawal. Alonso has since addressed that directly and called his involvement "a possibility," so the doubt is real but softer than stated. The striker's shirt remains the single biggest open question in the eleven.

The tactical read

Expect a 3-4-2-1, with Palmer and Rogers tucked inside behind João Pedro rather than holding width. Both of Friday's previews name that shape explicitly, which is a change from earlier in the week when the same personnel were being read variously as a 4-3-3 and a 4-2-3-1. The projected change sharpens it: with Lacroix in the back three and Hato at left wing-back, the three-at-the-back reading is now the clean one rather than an interpretation.

Lacroix's arrival changes the back line's character more than its quality. He is reportedly the fastest centre-back in the Premier League by top speed and is built to be the middle man of a three, which lets Fofana defend the right channel and Colwill stay left-footed on the left. Against a side that plays fast and direct, recovery pace in the middle of a back three is a specific answer to a specific problem.

The midfield pair remains the pressure point. Lavia and James screening a back three away from home is a lot to ask: Lavia has an injury history and James is a converted full-back in that role. If Caicedo is passed fit this is where he lands, and the balance of the whole team changes — he is the only natural holder in the building.

Set pieces are the tactical subplot, and Brentford are the worst possible opponent for it. Chelsea hired set-piece coach Austin MacPhee specifically to fix a known defensive weakness, and the long throw into a crowded box is a core part of how Brentford generate chances. A back three of Fofana, Lacroix and Colwill is tall and quick. Their own line is patched with Collins and van den Berg both out, which cuts both ways at dead balls. The projected home side is Kelleher behind Kayode, Schuster, Ajer and Lewis-Potter, with Janelt and Sangaré screening and Igor Thiago leading the line off Schade, Yarmoliuk and Anthony.

What to watch

Whether João Pedro is on the team sheet. He is the one genuine doubt in the projected eleven and the one with no like-for-like replacement — Welbeck is the named fallback but a different kind of forward. The official XI lands an hour before kickoff and settles it.

Whether Lacroix starts, and whether Gusto survives. This page changed its projection this morning on the strength of two matchday previews. The team sheet is the test of that call, and the right wing-back slot is the part most likely to embarrass it.

Whether Caicedo appears at all. Alonso moved him from "not yet" to "getting closer" inside a week and put the decision on this morning. Even a place on the bench would be the most meaningful team-news development of the season so far — a start would be bigger still.
