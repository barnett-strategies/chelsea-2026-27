Team news

Alonso ruled nobody out, and he has not moved off that. At Thursday's press conference — his last before kickoff — he said: "No-one is ruled out for us for Friday. I prefer to be cautious and we will wait until tomorrow to take the final decision." Every call goes to Friday's final training session. This page therefore goes into matchday carrying the same question marks it had on Wednesday, with two of them now pointing in opposite directions.

Reece James looks better than he did this morning. His absence from the open portion of Tuesday's session was described by Alonso as a decision rather than an injury — precautionary management, not a problem. He is treated here as likely to start.

João Pedro looks worse. He also missed Tuesday's visible training, and he has since withdrawn from the Brazil squad for the upcoming international window with an injury. A national-team withdrawal is harder evidence than a missed open session. Chelsea's own account still has him in contention and Alonso has not ruled him out, so he stays in the projected eleven — but he is now the single most likely name to drop out of it.

Caicedo's problem is a calf, and it dates to pre-season. Alonso moved him from "not at the moment to take risks with him" before matchweek 4 to "He's getting closer and tomorrow we will make the final decision" on Thursday. He has not started a match this season, so he stays out of the projection. Jordan Henderson (wrist), Marco Palestra and Emmanuel Emegha are all carried as fitness doubts, and the sources disagree about the first two: Chelsea's own site has Alonso suggesting both could debut, while The Chelsea Chronicle reports both as still unavailable. Treat a debut as possible, not expected.

Brentford are missing four and doubtful on a fifth. Nathan Collins (calf), Sepp van den Berg (groin), Josh Dasilva (thigh), Antoni Milambo (knee) and Kafe Furo are all listed out, with Mathias Jensen doubtful on fitness. Two of those are centre-backs. Keith Andrews' side also played a midweek cup tie on Tuesday, so rotation is likely on top of the absences.

What changed from the projection

The projected eleven has not moved all week, and Thursday gave no reason to move it. This is still the matchweek 4 side, repeated — the back three of Fofana, Colwill and Hato that Alonso picked ahead of Lacroix and Acheampong last time out. Sports Mole refreshed its team news at 8pm UK on Thursday, after the press conference, and its predicted side is unchanged from what it published on Wednesday. Nobody who has looked at this has changed their mind.

The two contested spots are both on the left and both are still open. Sports Mole projects Maxence Lacroix into the back three for Hato, and Pep Chavarría at left wing-back, which pushes Pedro Neto to the right and leaves Malo Gusto out. The other two previews on file keep Gusto right and Neto left. Still a 2-1 split, and the presser did nothing to break the tie.

Two corrections to what this page said earlier. This morning it framed Reece James and João Pedro as the same grade of doubt. They are not: James's Tuesday absence was precautionary, while João Pedro has since withdrawn from international duty injured. The risk sits almost entirely with the striker. Separately, Wednesday's version had Henderson and Palestra closing in on debuts; the sourcing is genuinely split on that, and the team news above now says so rather than picking the optimistic read.

The largest risk to this eleven remains fitness, not selection. The shape is the most secure thing on this page. The centre-forward is the least.

The tactical read

Expect the 3-4-3 Alonso used at Stamford Bridge last time, with Palmer and Rogers tucked inside behind João Pedro rather than holding width. Sports Mole reads the same personnel as a 4-3-3 and KhelNow as a 4-2-3-1 — less a disagreement than three descriptions of what the shape becomes once one wing-back pushes on and the other holds.

The midfield pair is the pressure point. As projected, Lavia and James screen a back three away from home against a side that plays fast and direct. Lavia has an injury history and James is a converted full-back in that role. If Caicedo is passed fit, this is where he lands and the balance of the whole team changes — he is the only natural holder in the building.

Brentford are set up to attack the channels and the box. The projected home side is Kelleher in goal behind a back four of Kayode, Schuster, Ajer and Lewis-Potter, with Janelt and Sangaré screening and Igor Thiago leading the line off Schade, Yarmoliuk and Anthony. With Collins and van den Berg both out, their own back line is patched — which cuts both ways at set pieces.

Set pieces are the tactical subplot. Chelsea hired set-piece coach Austin MacPhee specifically to fix a known defensive weakness, and Brentford are the worst possible opponent to still be working on it — the long throw into the box is a core part of how they generate chances. A taller back three of Fofana, Colwill and Hato helps.

What to watch

Whether João Pedro is on the team sheet. He is the one genuine doubt in the projected eleven and the one whose absence has no like-for-like replacement. The official XI lands an hour before kickoff and settles it.

Whether Caicedo appears at all. Alonso moved him from "not yet" to "getting closer" inside a week and put the decision on Friday morning. Even a place on the bench would be the most meaningful team-news development of the season so far — a start would be bigger still.

Which side Pedro Neto starts on, and Brentford's long throws. Neto switched left in matchweek 4 when Gusto came in; if Chavarría starts, Neto goes back right. And the crowded box is the clearest test yet of whether the MacPhee appointment has changed how Chelsea defend one.
