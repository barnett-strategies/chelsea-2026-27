Team news

Moises Caicedo is now rated 50/50 for this match, and the decision has still not landed. He is the only Chelsea player carrying a live doubt. The problem is a recurrence of the calf injury from the Brighton match, and Alonso has taken the blame for it directly: He has a problem in his calf. It was the same that he had before Brighton. Probably it was my mistake to push him too early, so that's why I'm cautious now not to make the same mistake. The weight of reporting now leans toward Chelsea playing it safe and holding him back for Brentford on 18 September. He has played 14 minutes all season.

Three players are out, and none of them is a surprise. Jordan Henderson is still one to two weeks from a return with the arm injury he carried out of the World Cup, so his Chelsea debut waits again. Emmanuel Emegha is out with a muscle problem. Marco Palestra has still not played this season, with Alonso flat about it: Still not ready to go. Not the moment to take too early a risk. Chelsea have no suspensions. Levi Colwill is fit, confirmed twice, and is expected to start.

Hull City have had a genuinely good week on availability, and this is the first proper opposition team news this page has carried. Paddy McNair is cleared to play after scans ruled out anything significant in the foot problem that kept him out of the midweek cup tie at Sunderland. Brooke Norton-Cuffy has also been passed fit after a scan. Timothy Iroegbunam is available again as his fitness builds. Sergej Jakirovic reports no fresh injuries from midweek.

Hull's absentee list is long, but most of it is long-term rather than new. Elliot Matazo (ruptured ACL, back around February), Darko Gyabi (groin, late November), Jack Butland (elbow, November), Ilyas Ansah (at least six weeks), Charlie Hughes (groin, mid-October) and Hidemasa Morita (groin, roughly two more weeks) are all out. The one live question is John Egan, who took a lower-leg knock in a collision at Sunderland. He finished that match and Jakirovic played the injury down afterwards, but he needs monitoring before Saturday.

The tactical read

The shape is unchanged: Alonso's settled 3-4-2-1, written by both source pages as a 3-4-3 - the same side described differently, with Palmer and Rogers as forwards rather than as free eights behind Joao Pedro.

With Caicedo at 50/50 and leaning out, the midfield pair picks itself. Alonso's own framing has James, Lavia and a fit Caicedo as three candidates for two places, so the projected Reece James alongside Romeo Lavia follows directly. Neto holds the right wing-back slot and Hato the left, meaning a winger and a converted defender supply the width against a side that will not give much of it away. The load question is still Lavia, whose seasons have been injury-hit and who has no recognised holding partner in reserve.

Hull's defensive record is the real problem here, and today's news makes it harder rather than easier. They have not conceded a league goal all season - three clean sheets in three, unbeaten, seven points - and are only the third promoted side in Premier League history to open a top-flight campaign that way. Getting McNair and Norton-Cuffy back through scans means Jakirovic can largely field the unit that produced it, even with Hughes and Morita unavailable. The one softness is Egan, whose lower-leg knock is unresolved.

That sharpens what Chelsea's setup actually has to solve. Against a settled low block that has not yet been breached, the first pass has to break a line rather than beat a press, which is where Colwill on the left of the three earns his place in possession as much as out of it. The market has Chelsea at a de-vigged 78 percent; Hull's record says the opening half-hour is likelier to be patient than emphatic.

What to watch

Whether Caicedo is in the squad at all. Friday's press conference at Cobham is where this resolves. The injury is a recurrence rather than a fresh knock, Alonso has publicly owned rushing him once already, and the honest reading now leans toward him waiting for Brentford on 18 September.

Whether Hull's clean sheet survives, and how Chelsea go about it. They arrive as the only side in the league yet to concede, with two defenders just passed fit. The gap between that record and a market pricing Chelsea at 78 percent is the most interesting tension in the fixture. Watch whether Chelsea probe patiently through the wing-backs or force it early through the middle.

Levi Colwill's first league start of the season, and how long he is given. He played four times in all of last season coming back from an ACL injury, and has had a fitness question raised and waved away inside the past week. Watch whether he sees out the full ninety or comes off as a precaution.
