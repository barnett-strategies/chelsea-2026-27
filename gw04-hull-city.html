Team news

Moisés Caicedo has hardened from an open question into a major doubt. Thursday's injury list has him down as exactly that. The problem is now specified as a recurrence of the calf injury he picked up against Brighton, which is why he was unavailable for both the Arsenal league trip and the midweek cup tie. This match is still listed as his earliest possible return, but some reporting has already moved his realistic date to 18 September against Brentford. He has played 14 minutes all season. Alonso's own framing has been consistent throughout - not the moment to take a too-early risk.

Three names are flatly out. Jordan Henderson (wrist, carried out of the World Cup) is now pointed at Brentford rather than this weekend, so his Chelsea debut waits again. Emmanuel Emegha is out with no return date attached. Marco Palestra has still not featured this season after a training-ground knock; this match remains the earliest he could appear, but he is listed as unavailable rather than doubtful, and a fortnight of reporting pointing him at this game has not yet turned into anything confirmed.

Levi Colwill is fit, and that is a genuine change in his favour rather than a neutral note. A fitness question was raised over him in midweek and Alonso has dismissed it directly: No, no. It is just from little things that he's having, but we don't expect that it's an injury. He is now expected to start rather than merely be available.

Chelsea have no suspensions for this fixture. The unsourced claim that Chelsea would be without Emiliano Martínez, checked and rejected on this page yesterday, has still produced no supporting injury or suspension report anywhere. He remains projected to start.

The tactical read

The shape is unchanged: Alonso's settled 3-4-2-1 - back three, two high wing-backs, a midfield pair, two free eights behind a lone striker. Nothing in this week's news points away from it.

Colwill's arrival tidies the back three rather than merely swapping a name into it. He is a natural left-sided centre-back, which lets Lacroix shift into the middle and keeps Acheampong on the right - a more orthodox handedness than the projection carried yesterday. It also puts a genuine ball-player on the left of the three, which matters against a deep block: the first pass out has to break a low line rather than beat a press.

Reece James stays in central midfield, and Caicedo's likely absence is why. With Caicedo a major doubt, the midfield two picks itself as James alongside Lavia, and the question yesterday's note raised - whether a fit Caicedo would push James back toward defence - simply does not arise this week. Neto therefore stays at right wing-back and Hato holds the left, so a winger and a converted defender supply the width.

The load question has moved from the defence to the midfield. Acheampong and Lacroix are into a third match in seven days, but the sharper issue is Lavia, whose season has been injury-hit and who now has no recognised holding partner in reserve if Caicedo is not passed fit.

What to watch

Whether Caicedo is named at all. Alonso said the call comes Thursday or Friday, so the Friday press conference is the moment this resolves. Even a green light after fourteen minutes of football all season is unlikely to mean ninety, and the honest reading now leans toward him waiting for Brentford.

Colwill's first league start of the season, and how he is managed. He played four times in all of last season coming back from an ACL, then had a fitness question raised in midweek that Alonso has waved away. Watch whether he lasts the full match or is taken off as a precaution.

How Chelsea break down a deep-lying five. Hull are expected to defend in a low block. At a de-vigged 78 percent the market expects that to be solved, so the things worth watching are patience, the quality of service from the wing-backs, and whether Palmer and Rogers can find space between the lines rather than in front of them.
