Team news

Moises Caicedo is out. Alonso confirmed it at Cobham on Friday, and the week's biggest open question is now closed. The manager's words on the club's own site were unambiguous: Not yet; let's see for Brentford, and it's not the moment to take the risk with him. The calf problem was a recurrence rather than a fresh injury, Alonso had already publicly owned pushing him back too early before Brighton, and he has now chosen caution. Caicedo ends the week having played 14 minutes all season.

Romeo Lavia is available, confirmed by Alonso directly. That was the other half of the midfield question and it has landed the right way. Levi Colwill is fit, with the fitness fears raised earlier in the week waved away, and is expected to start. Chelsea have no suspensions.

Three more are out, and Alonso is hoping for Brentford on all of them. Jordan Henderson (wrist) and Emmanuel Emegha (muscle) are both unavailable, with the manager offering only We hope they can be against Brentford, but we will see. Marco Palestra has still not played this season and remains sidelined. One loose end: one preview lists Valentin Barco among the absentees while another names him as a bench option, and nothing from the press conference settles it.

Hull City arrive in better shape than they were a week ago. Sergej Jakirovic confirmed on Friday that Paddy McNair is available after two weeks out and Brooke Norton-Cuffy has been given the all clear after missing the midweek cup tie at Sunderland. Timothy Iroegbunam is available again as his fitness builds.

Hull's absentee list is long but mostly long-term. Hidemasa Morita and Charlie Hughes stay out, alongside Elliot Matazo (ruptured ACL, back around February), Darko Gyabi (groin, late November), Jack Butland (elbow, November) and Ilyas Ansah (at least six weeks). John Egan, the one live doubt after a lower-leg knock in midweek, was not named among Friday's absentees, which points toward him being available — though that is inference from omission rather than a statement.

The tactical read

The shape is unchanged: Alonso's 3-4-2-1, written by some pages as a 3-4-3 — the same side described differently, with Palmer and Rogers as forwards rather than as free eights behind João Pedro.

The midfield is now settled by fact rather than by inference. Alonso's framing all week was that James, Lavia and a fit Caicedo were three candidates for two places; with Caicedo ruled out, the two pick themselves. What that exposes is the bench behind them. There is no recognised holding player in reserve — Malo Gusto is the listed midfield cover — so Lavia's load is the structural risk in this setup. His last two seasons have been injury-hit and he starts a two-man midfield with no like-for-like replacement.

The one genuine selection question left is what Chelsea want from the right of the back three. Acheampong is the younger, more front-footed option who has been in the side; Fofana is the more experienced body, and was written into the bench as defensive cover earlier in the week. Against a deep block the choice matters less defensively than it does in possession, where that centre-back will spend most of the afternoon as a spare man being invited to carry the ball forward.

Hull's defensive record is the actual problem, and it has not softened. They have not conceded a league goal all season — three clean sheets in three, unbeaten, seven points — and are only the third promoted side in Premier League history to open a top-flight campaign that way. With McNair and Norton-Cuffy cleared, Jakirovic can largely field the unit that produced it.

He was blunt about how they will do it. Jakirovic joked Kick them before making the serious point that man-marking is pointless against Chelsea, because they can pass beyond several players at once, so Hull will stay compact and defend as a unit. Against a block like that the first pass has to break a line rather than beat a press, which is where Colwill on the left of the three earns his place in possession as much as out of it. The market has Chelsea at a de-vigged 78 percent; Hull's record says the opening half-hour is likelier to be patient than emphatic.

What to watch

Which of Acheampong and Fofana starts at right centre-back. With Caicedo resolved, this is the last open selection question, and today's predicted elevens are split evenly on it. Every other name is agreed across all four sources consulted.

Romeo Lavia's minutes. He starts a two-man midfield with Caicedo out and no recognised holding player behind him on the bench. Watch how long he is given, and whether Chelsea reshape rather than replace him if he comes off.

Whether Hull's clean sheet survives, and how Chelsea go about breaking it. They arrive as the only side in the league yet to concede, with two defenders just passed fit and a manager who has said plainly they will defend as a compact unit. The gap between that and a market pricing Chelsea at 78 percent is the most interesting tension in the fixture — watch whether Chelsea probe patiently through the wing-backs or force it early through the middle.
