The situation

A new era opening in the least forgiving place

Chelsea finished 10th last season, went trophyless after losing the FA Cup final to Manchester City, burned through two managers (Enzo Maresca in January, Liam Rosenior in April), and are without European football for the first time in nine years. Xabi Alonso arrived on 1 July on a four-year deal after leaving Real Madrid in January, and inherits a squad that has now absorbed more than £340m of new spending across nine first-team signings this summer — our sum of the reported fees, and it is still not a closed window.

The opener is a west London derby, away, on a Monday night, in front of a full Craven Cottage — Sky's first Monday Night Football of the season. There is no soft landing here.

What it means: the fixture is a genuine read on whether Alonso's back-three model has bedded in during a five-match Asia-Pacific pre-season, with several starters only reporting late after the World Cup. Expect a cautious, control-first Chelsea rather than the free-flowing version people remember from Leverkusen.

The likely XI

Fofana's suspension pushes Reece James back into defence

Shape: 3-4-2-1 · Alonso started a back three again in the final friendly, so the system is now observed rather than assumed

The forced change: Wesley Fofana is suspended for this match. He was sent off for a second yellow at Sunderland on the final day of last season, and that one-game ban carries into the new campaign — pre-season friendlies don't clear it, which is why he could start against Real Sociedad but cannot start here. Fofana played right centre-back in that friendly, so his absence is the single biggest reason this XI differs from our July projection: Reece James moves out of the midfield two and into the back three, and Pedro Neto comes in on the flank. Also unavailable: Mamadou Sarr, Aaron Anselmino and Emanuel Emegha, all injured.
And the change that didn't happen: Enzo Fernández is staying, and is expected to start. The concrete interest was Manchester City, not Real Madrid. Chelsea set City a hard deadline of 5:00 PM UK on Friday 14 August to bid against a £120m valuation; City never bid. Chelsea have since said they will not sell him this window or in January, and the verbal terms offered to suitors in May are off the table. He came off the bench at 62 minutes against Real Sociedad to a genuinely mixed reception — booed by some of the Bridge, cheered by others. Fabrizio Romano still won't call it dead before the window shuts on 1 September, so treat this as settled-for-now rather than settled.

Depth and open questions

What could change this XI

Genuine competition

Attack: Estêvão (41), Jamie Gittens (11) and new arrival Geovany Quenda all came into contention off the bench in pre-season. Emanuel Emegha — the Strasbourg captain whose move was pre-agreed back in September 2025 — is injured and unavailable for this match. Alejandro Garnacho has left on loan.

Midfield: Romeo Lavia (45) is fit and back in the mix, having come off the bench against Real Sociedad; Dário Essugo (14) is behind him. If Alonso wants Reece James in midfield rather than defence, Lavia is the man who makes that possible. Andrey Santos was sold to Manchester United for around £50m, which thinned this group more than expected.

Defence and the left side: Jorrel Hato (21) drops to the bench in this projection, and Chelsea did finally buy the specialist left-sided option they were hunting: Pep Chavarría (29), 28, signed from Rayo Vallecano in August for £16.3m plus £1.7m in add-ons on a deal to 2031 — 33 La Liga appearances and 11 clean sheets last season for a Rayo side that reached the Conference League final. Also available: Malo Gusto (27), Trevoh Chalobah (23), Tosin Adarabioyo (4), Josh Acheampong (34), Benoît Badiashile. Out: Fofana (3, suspended), Mamadou Sarr (19) and Aaron Anselmino (30), both injured.

Who was left out: Nicolás Jackson, Liam Delap and Tosin all missed the final friendly, which reporting took as a signal about who Alonso expects to be gone before the window shuts on 1 September.

Two of July's three questions are now answered. The back three is real — Alonso has used it repeatedly, including in the final friendly. Enzo Fernández is staying and should start. That leaves the genuine unknowns: how Chelsea cope with Fofana banned and James pulled out of midfield to replace him, whether Palestra or Neto takes the left, and how sharp the late World Cup returnees are after only five training sessions together as a group. What to watch for: if Alonso instead picks Chalobah or Acheampong at right centre-back and leaves James in midfield, that tells you he rates the midfield pairing above the defensive one — the most informative single decision on the teamsheet.
